        Building your tree

        Gap › in Build goes to

        A gap is a position you'll reach without an answer: your move missing, an opponent reply you haven't prepared, or a line that stops. Move by move grows the tree evenly: every gap at move 2 first, then move 3, then 4, biggest first within a move. Most frequent goes to the one you'll meet most often, wherever it is, so it can go deep down a popular line. Rating impact weighs that by how far below 50% you'd score there, from your own games blended with peer data.

        Which gaps

        Played against me only counts what your own games reached: opponent replies you've actually faced, and positions you've actually had. And good also drops replies that were mistakes (worse than the good margin in Advanced) in every game you met them, so you don't prep against blunders. It needs your games analysed (Games > Analyse all).

        Every line has to stop somewhere, so gaps after this move don't count against an opening. Covered then means: the share of games at your level through that opening that are still in your prep after this move.

        Lets you build a line quickly by only playing your own moves. Needs the Lichess token.

        Engine and games

        The blend works like this: with n games of your own, your expected score is (n × yours + this number × peers) ÷ (n + this number). Sample sizes are shown next to every statistic.

        Hidden gems and better moves

        A hidden gem must pass all three. A "better" move must have the minimum games and score that many points more than your move. Small samples are noisy, so keep the game count high.

        Repertoire backup

        Export writes the colour selected at the top as PGN with variations. Import adds every move in a pasted PGN to that colour, variations included. Nothing is removed: a different move of yours where you already have one becomes another option.
